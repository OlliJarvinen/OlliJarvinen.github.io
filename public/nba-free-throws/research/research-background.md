# Research background and contribution

Reviewed before choosing the models on 16 October 2026.

## Cao, Price & Stone (2011)

[Performance Under Pressure in the NBA](https://doi.org/10.1177/1527002511404785), Journal of Sports Economics 12(3), 231–252. [Author manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Daniel-Stone-10/publication/227574446_Performance_Under_Pressure_in_the_NBA/links/02bfe51325cd819a54000000/Performance-Under-Pressure-in-the-NBA.pdf).

Their 2002–03 to 2009–10 analysis uses player-season fixed effects, quarter indicators and FT-set/previous-outcome controls, with time-by-score categories. It reports a 5–10 percentage-point decline in the very final seconds of close games, with exceptions including tied games in the final 15 seconds. We treat that as a specific conditional result, not a universal pressure penalty. Shooter selection, intentional fouling and unobserved changes within player-seasons complicate causal interpretation.

## Goldman & Rao (2012)

[Effort vs. Concentration: The Asymmetric Impact of Pressure on NBA Performance](https://assets-global.website-files.com/5f1af76ed86d6771ad48324b/655553752b90bfcd293461d1_SSAC12%20-%2016-Goldman_Rao_Sloan2012_updated.pdf), MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference.

They already connect point value in win-probability terms to performance. Thus **win-probability leverage itself is not novel**. This project tests finite make-minus-miss contrasts using modern observations, explicit remaining-attempt state, and chronological incremental-prediction comparisons. It does not claim to invent pressure measurement.

## Yaari & Eisenmann (2011)

[The Hot (Invisible?) Hand](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0024532), PLOS ONE 6(10), e24532. Their analysis demonstrates how aggregated FT patterns can reflect heterogeneity across players and time, and motivates controlling for attempt order and the preceding outcome. Serial association alone does not establish psychological feedback.

## What the investigation can contribute

- A ten-season contemporary descriptive sample with auditable exclusions.
- Shooter expectations estimated strictly from completed earlier games.
- Sequence-aware leverage constructed from earlier-season win models.
- An explicit comparison of leverage with both coarse clutch labels and flexible time–score controls on later seasons.
- Cluster-aware uncertainty and sensitivity to shooter-baseline and leverage specifications.

The eventual report must distinguish agreement with these findings, methodological differences, and evidence of additional predictive information. Observational associations will not be described as causal pressure effects.
